Limitations of these models

  • The one-compartment model treats the body as a single well-mixed volume. Many drugs distribute in ways that need two or more compartments.
  • Elimination is assumed first-order (rate proportional to concentration). Some drugs show saturable, non-linear elimination at higher concentrations.
  • Parameters are fixed here; in people they change over time and differ widely between individuals.
  • The Bateman function assumes first-order absorption and a single dose. Real absorption can have lag times, be erratic or be limited by dissolution.
  • AUC is computed here from a mathematical formula, and the table uses the trapezoidal rule. Real studies extrapolate from sampled points and carry measurement error.
