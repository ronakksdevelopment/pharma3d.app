AI in pharmacy

Artificial intelligence is a set of computational methods that can assist with certain pharmacy tasks. This page explains six areas of interest and ten practical applications, with attention to what each needs, what it may support, and where it falls short.

EVIDENCE VARIES BY TOPIC EACH TOPIC SHOWS ITS OWN TIER

How to read this page. AI tools can assist, may support and have potential to help with defined tasks, and they require validation and human oversight. They do not guarantee outcomes, are not always correct, do not replace pharmacists and do not remove the need for professional judgement. Examples are general descriptions, not endorsements of any product.

Principles that apply to every topic

Requires validation

An AI model should be tested on data that reflect where it will be used, before and after deployment. Performance in one setting may not carry over to another.

Requires human oversight

Qualified people remain responsible for decisions. AI output can be wrong, incomplete or out of date, and should be checked.

Data quality and bias

Models reflect the data they learn from. Missing, biased or unrepresentative data can lead to unreliable or unfair results.

Privacy and governance

Patient data need appropriate consent, protection and legal compliance. Regulation of AI in health is evolving and differs between countries.

Evidence and limits

Evidence tiers are assigned per topic and reflect how well established the use is, in general terms. They are editorial judgements for education and should be checked against current primary literature and regulator guidance. Content should be reviewed by a qualified pharmacy informatics or clinical AI specialist before public release.
