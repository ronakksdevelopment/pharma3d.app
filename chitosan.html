Chitosan nanoparticles

A guided path through how a natural-polymer-derived material can be formed into crosslinked nanoparticles by ionic gelation, how drug can associate with it, how the result is characterised, and how release data are modelled. Everything here is conceptual and educational.

EVIDENCE TIER: EMERGING / RESEARCH-DEPENDENT NOT A FORMULATION RECIPE

Read this first. Chitosan is a family of materials, not one substance. Its behaviour depends on source, molecular weight, degree of deacetylation, purity and the exact preparation conditions. Findings from one study do not transfer automatically to another. No statement on this page should be read as a universal rule, a dosing statement or a claim that any product works clinically.

Stage 1 of 7

1 · Chitosan

The starting material.

What

Chitosan is a linear polysaccharide obtained by partially deacetylating chitin, a structural polymer found in crustacean shells and some fungi. It is a copolymer of glucosamine and N-acetylglucosamine units. The fraction of glucosamine units is described by the degree of deacetylation (DD).

How

Free amino groups on glucosamine units can accept a proton in mildly acidic solution, giving the polymer a positive charge and letting it dissolve. At higher pH these groups lose that charge and the polymer becomes much less soluble.

Why it matters

The positive charge in acid is what makes electrostatic crosslinking with negatively charged species possible. Chitosan is also studied for biocompatibility and biodegradability, although these properties depend on the grade and have to be tested for each material.

Limitations

Chitosan varies between suppliers and batches. Molecular weight, DD and residual protein or metal content differ. It is soluble only in acidic conditions, which limits some uses. Regulatory status and suitability depend on grade and intended route.

What

Nanoparticle formation is the point at which dissolved chitosan and polyanion interact to give a dispersed particulate phase instead of a clear solution or a bulk gel.

How

When the two solutions meet under stirring, local electrostatic interaction can create crosslinked domains that grow into particles. Whether particles, aggregates or a gel result depends on concentration, ratio, pH, mixing and other conditions.

Why

A crosslinked network can hold drug within or on the polymer matrix and keeps the system dispersed as small particles rather than a bulk material.

Limitations

Particles can aggregate, change with storage, or form a wide size distribution. The picture above is a teaching device; real particles are irregular and their internal structure is not settled by a diagram.

4 · Drug loading

Conceptual association of drug with the polymer network, then two functional calculators.

What

Drug loading describes how drug becomes associated with the particles. Drug can be entrapped within the network, adsorbed at the surface, or interact with polymer groups. Which of these dominates is a research question for each drug and each preparation.

How

In many reports the drug is dissolved in one of the solutions before mixing, so it is present as particles form. Charge, solubility and size of the drug influence how much associates.

Why

Association with a particle can change how a drug is released and handled. Whether this improves any clinical outcome must be shown by appropriate studies, not assumed.

Limitations

Some drug may sit on the surface and release quickly. Loading measured by an indirect method can hide losses. Highly water-soluble small molecules are often hard to retain.

USER CALCULATED

Encapsulation efficiency (EE%)

EE% = [(Wtotal − Wfree) / Wtotal] × 100
Wtotal
Total amount of drug initially added to the preparation.
Wfree
Amount of drug measured in the supernatant after separating the particles (unencapsulated, or not associated with the particles).
EE%
Percentage of the initial drug that is associated with the particles, by this indirect method.

Indirect method: assumes all drug missing from the supernatant is associated with the particles and that the assay is accurate. Drug degraded or lost elsewhere would be counted as encapsulated.

USER CALCULATED

Drug loading (DL%)

DL% = [(Wtotal − Wfree) / Wnp] × 100
Wnp
Mass of the recovered nanoparticles (polymer, crosslinker and associated drug), usually after drying.
DL%
Mass of associated drug as a percentage of total particle mass. Different papers define DL differently, so check the definition before comparing values.

Definition used here: drug associated per total particle mass. Some sources divide by polymer mass only.

7 · Potential application

Where researchers have proposed using chitosan-based particles, and how to read those proposals.

What

Chitosan nanoparticles have been studied as carriers in research areas such as mucosal delivery, ocular and nasal routes, and delivery of poorly permeable molecules. Most of this is preclinical.

How

The positive surface charge may promote interaction with negatively charged mucus and cell membranes, and chitosan has been reported to influence epithelial permeability in some experimental systems.

Why

These properties are the reason for research interest. Interest is not evidence of benefit for a given patient or drug.

Limitations

Results in cell cultures and animals often do not carry over to people. Scale-up, sterility, stability, batch consistency and regulatory review remain major hurdles. Application claims must be read against study design and the exact material used.
